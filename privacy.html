Privacy policy

Your trains, not your data.

Choo! has no accounts, no ads and no analytics. It doesn’t collect personal data. To find the railway and its trains, the app sends the Choo server some places on the map, including where you are while the map is open, and the server doesn’t keep them. This page says exactly what the app sends, to whom, and when.

Last updated 12 November 2026. Applies to the Choo! iPhone app (including its widgets and Live Activity), the Choo server it talks to, and this website.

The short version

  • No accounts. There's nothing to sign up for, and Choo! never asks for your name, email or phone number.
  • No ads, analytics or tracking. The app contains no advertising or analytics code and doesn't use Apple's advertising identifier.
  • Crash reports, only if you share them with app developers. If Choo! crashes or freezes and you've chosen to share analytics with app developers in your iPhone's settings, iOS gives the app a technical report, which it sends to the Choo server so the problem can be fixed (see below). It contains nothing about you.
  • Your location is used to find the railway beside you. On your phone, it shows the spots near you and keeps a Lock Screen Live Activity up to date. While the map is open, the app sends the Choo server where you are, to about 100 metres, to get the railway tracks around you, so it can offer Trains Here when you’re beside one. While Trains Here is open, it sends where you’re standing, rounded to about 10 metres, to find the trains passing there. To show trains moving on the map, it sends the part of the map on screen, rounded to about half a kilometre. The server doesn’t record any of these.
  • Your spots are kept on your phone. Where each one is goes with the requests for its trains, but not its name. If you follow one on your Lock Screen, the server keeps it only until that Live Activity ends. If you have unusual-train notifications on, the server keeps the spots they’re on for, with their names, so it can send them (see below). Your spots also go with an issue report if you send one.
  • Issue reports are only sent when you tap Send. They help put things right, and contain no account or contact details.

Who we are

Choo! is made by Andrew Bennet, an independent developer in the UK, who is responsible for the data described here. You can get in touch at hello@choo.express.

What stays on your phone

  • Your location. If you allow it, Choo! uses your location on the phone to show where you are on the map, to show and refresh the spots near you, and, while you follow a spot on your Lock Screen, to keep that Live Activity running and up to date as you walk there. To find the railway and its trains, it also sends the Choo server where you are, and the part of the map you’re looking at, as described below. It’s only sent while Choo! is on screen, and not to anyone else. You can turn it off in Settings › Privacy & Security › Location Services; Choo! still works, but won’t know which spots are near you.
  • Your spots (their names, where they are and how they’re set up). They’re kept in the app and shared with its own widgets. Where each one is, and the lines chosen for it, go with the requests for its trains (below).
  • Notifications about unusual trains are sent by the Choo server through Apple’s push service, whenever one is due, if you allow notifications and have them on. For that, the server keeps your phone’s push token and the spots they’re on for (below). Turn them off in Choo!’s Settings, or for one spot with Unusual-Train Alerts in its menu, and the server deletes them.
  • A short recording for issue reports. So that a problem can be looked into afterwards, the app keeps the last two hours of what it received from the server and what it showed on screen (up to about 20 MB), in its caches on your phone. Older parts are deleted as it goes. It only leaves your phone if you send an issue report.

Deleting the app deletes all of this.

What the app sends, and to whom

To show train times, the app has to ask for them. These requests are about trains and places on the railway. Some are about where you are: the tracks around you while the map is open, and where you’re standing while Trains Here is open. They don’t include your name or any identifier for you or your phone, apart from the push tokens for a followed Live Activity or for unusual-train notifications (below). The Choo server doesn’t keep a log of them, and doesn’t write the places asked about to disk.

IP addresses

Like any request on the internet, each one carries your internet (IP) address, which the service receiving it can see. The Choo server uses it only to limit how many requests one address can make, so the service stays up for everyone. It's held only in the server's memory for that, never logged, and discarded when the server restarts or needs the room. The exceptions: while you follow a spot on your Lock Screen, or have unusual-train notifications on, the server keeps a short hash of the address with them, on its disk, to limit how many one address can have, and deletes it with them. The server doesn't keep a log of the requests themselves. Fly.io, which hosts the server, processes requests to deliver them, under its own privacy policy. TfL, OpenStreetMap's servers, Wikipedia and Apple see your IP address when the app contacts them, under their own policies.

Issue reports

If something looks wrong, you can choose "Report an Issue" (from Settings, from a spot or a train, or by shaking your phone if you’ve turned that on). Nothing is sent until you write a note and tap Send. If it can't be sent straight away (no signal on a footbridge, say), it's saved on your phone and sent the next time Choo! opens.

A report contains:

  • the last two hours of what the app received from the Choo server (and from TfL, if it asked) and what it showed on screen, including when you opened spots and trains, and the positions of spots, of the map and, if you used Trains Here, of where you were standing, that those requests already contained;
  • your note, and the spot and train it's about, if you pick them;
  • a screenshot of the app, unless you choose "None";
  • your saved spots, with their names, where they are and how they're set up;
  • the app's version and build, your iPhone's model, its iOS version, its language, region and time zone setting, and whether Choo! is allowed to use your location (not where you are).

It doesn’t contain your location as such, an account, your name or contact details, unless you type them into the note. Please don’t. But the screenshot can show where you are on the map, and the requests above can show roughly where you were: the parts of the map, if it was following you, and the places Trains Here asked about.

Reports are kept on the Choo server's disk, as sent, so they can be read to find and fix problems. They're read only for that. When all the reports kept together pass 300 MB, the oldest are deleted to make room. They aren't shared with anyone else. If you'd like a report deleted sooner, email us with roughly when you sent it and what your note said, and we'll delete it.

Crash and performance reports

If you've turned on Share With App Developers (in your iPhone's Settings, under Privacy & Security, then Analytics & Improvements), iOS gives each app a technical report when it crashes, freezes, or uses an unusual amount of battery or storage, usually the next day. Choo! sends these to the Choo server so we can find and fix the problem. If you haven't turned it on, iOS gives the app nothing and nothing is sent.

A report contains where in Choo!'s code the problem happened, the kind of problem, and the app's version and build, your iPhone's model, its iOS version and its region setting. It doesn't contain your location, your spots, your name or any identifier for you or your phone. Reports are kept on the Choo server's disk for at most 60 days, read only to fix problems, and not shared with anyone else.

Children

Choo! is made for grown-ups to use with children, not for children to use on their own. It doesn't collect personal data from anyone, children included.

Emails from Choo!

If you leave your email address on this website to hear from Choo!, it's sent to Buttondown, the service we use to send those emails, under its privacy policy. Buttondown emails you first to confirm it's your address; until you confirm, you won't get anything else. We use your address only to email you about Choo! (when it's out, then the odd blog post and news), never share or sell it, and you can unsubscribe with the link in any email, after which it's deleted. Nothing about the app is linked to it.

This website

This website has no cookies, no analytics and no tracking, and loads nothing from other companies' servers: no fonts, scripts or embeds. The sign-up form sends your email address to Buttondown only when you submit it. The animations run in your browser and send nothing back. The site is hosted by GitHub Pages: GitHub sees your IP address when your browser asks for a page, as any web host does, and logs it for security, as its privacy statement describes.

Your rights

Under UK data protection law you can ask what personal data we hold about you, and ask us to correct or delete it. In practice the only things we might hold are your email address, if you signed up for emails, and an issue report you chose to send (crash reports contain nothing that could identify you), which we can find from when you sent it and what it said. Email hello@choo.express. If you're unhappy with how we've handled your data, you can complain to the Information Commissioner's Office.
