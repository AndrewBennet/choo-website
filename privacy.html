Privacy policy

Your trains, not your data.

Choo has no accounts, no ads and no analytics. It doesn’t collect personal data. To find the railway and its trains, the app sends the Choo server some places on the map, including roughly where you are while the map is open (if you’ve let it use your location), and the server doesn’t keep them. This page says exactly what the app sends, to whom, and when.

Last updated 18 November 2026. Applies to the Choo app for iPhone and iPad (including its widgets and Live Activities), the Choo server it talks to, and this website.

The short version

  • No accounts. There’s nothing to sign up for, and Choo never asks for your name, email or phone number.
  • No ads, analytics or tracking. The app contains no advertising or analytics code and doesn’t use Apple’s advertising identifier.
  • Crash reports, only if you share them with app developers. If Choo crashes or freezes and you’ve chosen to share analytics with app developers in your device’s settings, iOS gives the app a technical report, which it sends to the Choo server so the problem can be fixed (see below). It contains nothing about you.
  • Your location is used to find the railway beside you. On your device, it shows the spots near you and keeps a Lock Screen Live Activity up to date. While the map is open, the app sends the Choo server where you are, to about 100 metres, to get the railway tracks around you, so it can offer Trains Here when you’re beside one. While Trains Here is open, it sends where you’re standing, rounded to about 10 metres, to find the trains passing there. To show trains moving on the map, it sends the part of the map on screen, rounded to about half a kilometre. The server doesn’t record any of these. To show how long the walk to a spot is, the app asks Apple Maps, which gets where you are and where the spot is.
  • Your spots are kept on your device. Where each one is goes with the requests for its trains, but not its name. If you follow one on your Lock Screen, the server keeps it only until that Live Activity ends. If you have unusual-train notifications on, the server keeps the spots they’re on for, with their names, so it can send them (see below). Your spots also go with an issue report if you send one.
  • Issue reports are only sent if you send one. They’re used only to fix problems, and don’t say who you are.

Who we are

Choo is made by Andrew Bennet, an independent developer in the UK. You can get in touch at hello@choo.express, including to ask what we hold about you, or to correct or delete it. If you’re unhappy with how we’ve handled your data, you can complain to the Information Commissioner’s Office.

What stays on your device

  • Your location. If you allow it, Choo uses your location on your device to show where you are on the map, to show and refresh the spots near you, and, while you follow a spot on your Lock Screen, to keep that Live Activity running and up to date as you walk there. To find the railway and its trains, it also sends the Choo server roughly where you are, and the part of the map you’re looking at, and it asks Apple Maps for walking times, as described below. It’s only sent while Choo is on screen, and to no one else. You can turn it off in Settings › Privacy & Security › Location Services; Choo still works, but won’t know which spots are near you.
  • Your spots (their names, where they are and how they’re set up). They’re kept in the app and shared with its own widgets. Where each one is, and the lines chosen for it, go with the requests for its trains (below).
  • Notifications about unusual trains are sent by the Choo server through Apple’s push service, whenever one is due, if you allow notifications and have them on. For that, the server keeps your device’s push token and the spots they’re on for (below). Turn them off in Choo’s Settings, or for one spot with Unusual-Train Alerts in its menu, and the server deletes them.
  • A record of the last two hours. So a problem can be looked into after it happens, the app keeps a note of what the server sent it and what it showed on screen over the last two hours (up to about 20 MB), on your device. Anything older is deleted. It’s only sent if you send an issue report.

Deleting the app deletes all of this.

What the app sends, and to whom

To show train times, the app has to ask for them. These requests are about trains and places on the railway. Some are about where you are: the tracks around you while the map is open, and where you’re standing while Trains Here is open. They don’t include your name or any identifier for you or your device, apart from the push tokens and the random id used for a followed Live Activity or for unusual-train notifications (below). The Choo server doesn’t keep a log of them, and doesn’t write the places asked about to disk.

IP addresses

Like any request on the internet, each one carries your internet (IP) address, which the service receiving it can see. The Choo server uses it only to limit how many requests one address can make, so the service stays up for everyone. It’s held only in the server’s memory for that, never logged, and discarded when the server restarts or needs the room. The exceptions: while you follow a spot or a train on your Lock Screen, or have unusual-train notifications on, the server keeps a salted hash of the address with them, on its disk, to limit how many one address can have, and deletes it with them. The server doesn’t keep a log of the requests themselves. The company that hosts the server processes requests only to deliver them. TfL, Wikipedia and Apple see your IP address when the app contacts them, under their own policies.

Issue reports

If something looks wrong, you can choose "Report an Issue" (from Settings, from a spot or a train, or by shaking your device if you’ve turned that on). Nothing is sent until you write a note and tap Send. If it can’t be sent straight away (no signal on a footbridge, say), it’s saved on your device and sent the next time Choo opens.

A report contains:

  • the last two hours of what the app received from the Choo server (and from TfL, if it asked) and what it showed on screen, including when you opened spots and trains, and the positions of spots, of the map and, if you used Trains Here, of where you were standing, that those requests already contained;
  • your note, the kind of report you chose, and the spot and train it’s about, if you pick them;
  • a screenshot of the app, unless you choose "None";
  • your saved spots, with their names, where they are and how they’re set up;
  • the app’s settings, such as the map’s look, your notification settings and which spots have them off, and whether Reduce Motion is on;
  • the app’s version and build, your device’s model, its iOS version, its language, region and time zone setting, and whether Choo is allowed to use your location (not where you are).

It doesn’t contain your location as such, an account, your name or contact details, unless you type them into the note. Please don’t. But the screenshot can show where you are on the map, and the requests above can show roughly where you were: the parts of the map, if it was following you, and the places Trains Here asked about.

Reports are kept on the Choo server’s disk, as sent, so they can be read to find and fix problems. They’re read only for that, and deleted after 60 days. To help read them, reports may be looked over by an AI assistant. They aren’t shared with anyone else. If you’d like a report deleted sooner, email us with roughly when you sent it and what your note said, and we’ll delete it.

Crash and performance reports

If you’ve turned on Share With App Developers (in your device’s Settings, under Privacy & Security, then Analytics & Improvements), iOS gives each app a technical report when it crashes, freezes, or uses an unusual amount of battery or storage, usually the next day. Choo sends these to the Choo server so we can find and fix the problem. If you haven’t turned it on, iOS gives the app nothing and nothing is sent.

A report contains where in Choo’s code the problem happened, the kind of problem, and the app’s version and build, your device’s model, its iOS version and its region setting. It doesn’t contain your location, your spots, your name or any identifier for you or your device. Reports are kept for at most 60 days, and are read only to fix problems, in the same way as issue reports. They aren’t shared with anyone else.

Children

Choo is made for grown-ups to use with children, not for children to use on their own. It doesn’t collect personal data from anyone, children included.

Emails from Choo

If you leave your email address on this website to hear from Choo, it’s sent to Buttondown, the service we use to send those emails, under its privacy policy. Like any website, Buttondown also sees your IP address and browser when the form is sent. Buttondown emails you first to confirm it’s your address; until you confirm, you won’t get anything else. We use your address only to email you about Choo (when it’s out, then the odd blog post and news), and never share or sell it. You can unsubscribe with the link in any email, and we won’t email you again; if you’d like your address deleted altogether, email us and we will. Nothing about the app is linked to it.

This website

This website has no cookies and doesn’t track you. To see how many people visit, it uses Plausible, an EU-based counter that doesn’t use cookies or store your IP address, and counts visits without knowing who you are: which page, the site that sent you, and your rough country, browser and device type. It also counts sign-ups and links followed off the site. Read Plausible’s data policy. Apart from that it loads nothing from other companies’ servers: no fonts or embeds. The sign-up form sends your email address to Buttondown only when you submit it. The animations run in your browser and send nothing back. Like any web host, the one that serves this site sees your IP address when your browser asks for a page.

Changes

If Choo ever starts sending anything new, this page will change first, and the date at the top will say when.
